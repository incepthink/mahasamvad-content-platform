# Proposal for a Complete AI-Powered PR Solution for DGIPR

## End-to-End Content Production, Media Intelligence, Multilingual Communication and On-Site Operational Support

> **From official source material to finished, review-ready public communication—delivered through a secure, on-premises AI environment.**

## 1. Purpose of the Proposal

The Directorate General of Information and Public Relations (DGIPR) manages a wide range of public-communication responsibilities: news and article production, social-media communication, translation, proofreading, media monitoring, studio interviews, video production, campaign analysis and the preservation of institutional knowledge. These activities require accuracy, speed, consistent Marathi, correct official terminology and a reliable government visual identity.

HashCase proposes a **Complete AI-Powered Public Relations Solution** that combines:

- a secure, on-premises AI platform developed for DGIPR;
- one or two trained HashCase professionals working on-site at a DGIPR-designated office location;
- end-to-end production of finished, review-ready outputs;
- specialised workflows for articles, translations, creatives, videos, interviews, media intelligence, presentations and RFPs; and
- continuous training, quality control and identification of new opportunities for AI-assisted departmental work.

This is an output-oriented service, not merely the provision of a software tool. HashCase personnel will use the platform to process the material supplied by DGIPR, prepare the required deliverables, perform first-level quality checks and submit the completed output for departmental review. DGIPR officers will retain final factual, administrative and publication approval.

## 2. Executive Summary

DGIPR receives information in many forms: meeting notes, government resolutions, press notes, circulars, audio recordings, WhatsApp voice notes, YouTube videos, photographed letters, scanned PDFs, newspaper pages, studio recordings and previously published Mahasamvad articles. Converting this material into accurate communication currently requires repeated transcription, extraction, rewriting, translation, design, editing and reformatting.

The proposed solution brings these activities into one secure AI Newsroom and managed content-production operation. A single approved source can be transformed into a Marathi news report, feature article, translation, social-media creative, caption, article poster, YouTube thumbnail, presentation, short video, studio programme, blog post or analytical report without recreating the assignment for every format.

The foundation of the solution will be a **department-controlled on-premises AI environment**. Approved Large Language Models (LLMs), speech and vision models, the DGIPR knowledge base, document embeddings, vector database and core processing services will operate within DGIPR-controlled infrastructure or an approved government data centre. **Semantic Search**, **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)**, **model fine-tuning**, source provenance and AI evaluation will enable the system to use authorised departmental knowledge while preserving data sovereignty and human approval.

The result will be a complete PR production capability in which technology performs repeatable high-volume work, HashCase personnel manage production and quality, and DGIPR officers concentrate on reviewing and approving final communication.

## 3. Complete PR Solution for DGIPR

The proposed solution covers the complete journey from source material to public communication:

1. Receive official notes, documents, recordings, links, images, data or raw studio footage.
2. Digitise and organise the material through OCR, speech-to-text and multimodal document understanding.
3. Retrieve relevant authorised information through Semantic Search and RAG.
4. Verify names, designations, dates, amounts, schemes and other sensitive facts.
5. Produce the required written, visual, audio, video or analytical output.
6. Apply departmental language, templates, branding and terminology.
7. Perform first-level editorial, factual, visual and technical quality checks.
8. Submit a finished, review-ready output to DGIPR.
9. Incorporate departmental corrections and provide the final approved version.
10. Preserve reusable outputs, references and institutional knowledge in a searchable archive.

## 4. Output-Based Managed Service Model

Under the proposed model, HashCase will take responsibility for operating the AI production platform and preparing final outputs. DGIPR employees will not be required to independently operate every feature or manage the underlying AI workflows.

### 4.1 HashCase responsibilities

- Operate the AI Newsroom and its specialised production tools.
- Receive and organise source material supplied by DGIPR.
- Transcribe recordings and extract content from documents.
- Prepare news, articles, translations, creatives, videos, reports, presentations and RFP drafts.
- Conduct first-level source, language, terminology, layout and technical checks.
- Submit review-ready outputs to the designated DGIPR officers.
- Implement corrections communicated during departmental review.
- Train DGIPR personnel who wish to use the available self-service tools.
- Identify repetitive departmental tasks that can be simplified through new AI workflows.
- Coordinate platform improvements, technical support and output-quality monitoring.

### 4.2 DGIPR responsibilities

- Provide the official source material, instructions and required communication objective.
- Clarify policy or factual matters that are not established by the available source.
- Review the completed output.
- Communicate required corrections or approvals.
- Retain final authority over factual acceptance, official approval and publication.

## 5. Secure On-Premises AI Foundation

Government communications may contain sensitive, unpublished or policy-related information. The proposed solution will therefore be built on an **on-premises, private AI architecture** instead of depending solely on uncontrolled public AI applications.

The private environment will be hosted within DGIPR-controlled infrastructure or an approved government data centre. Source documents, recordings, extracted text, embeddings, generated outputs and operational records can remain within the authorised environment under government-defined access and retention policies.

### 5.1 Private AI inference

Approved language, vision, speech and document-intelligence models can be deployed within the on-premises environment. Locally hosted LLMs will support drafting, summarisation, question answering, classification and assisted review. Marathi-capable speech-to-text, Optical Character Recognition (OCR) and multimodal models will process recordings, scans, photographs and complex documents.

This provides DGIPR with control over:

- which models are approved for departmental work;
- where inference takes place;
- where source material and generated output are stored;
- how long information is retained;
- who can access each system and dataset; and
- how models, prompts and knowledge indexes are updated.

Any external service or integration will be used only when specifically approved by DGIPR and permitted by the applicable security policy.

### 5.2 Private departmental knowledge base

Authorised Mahasamvad articles, government resolutions, circulars, scheme documents, terminology, approved templates and other departmental records will form a private knowledge base. Documents will be cleaned, segmented into meaningful chunks and converted into numerical **embeddings** that represent their semantic meaning.

These embeddings will be stored in a department-controlled **vector database**, allowing the system to retrieve material by meaning and context rather than only by exact keyword. This **Semantic Search** layer will support historical research, related-article discovery, fact-checking, style matching and question answering across the DGIPR archive.

### 5.3 Retrieval-Augmented Generation

The platform will use **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)** to ground AI outputs in authorised information. For each task, the retrieval layer will identify the most relevant documents or passages and provide them to the model as controlled context.

RAG will help the system:

- locate relevant government and Mahasamvad material;
- cite the supporting source for important claims;
- distinguish current facts from historical style references;
- reduce unsupported or hallucinated information;
- update departmental knowledge without retraining the complete model; and
- preserve traceability between the original source and the generated output.

Official documents will remain the factual source of truth. The AI model will not be treated as an independent factual database.

### 5.4 Model fine-tuning and domain adaptation

Where supported by approved datasets and formal evaluation, selected models can undergo **fine-tuning**, **instruction tuning** or other forms of domain adaptation for DGIPR's requirements. Adaptation may cover:

- Mahasamvad editorial style and article structure;
- Marathi government terminology;
- common departmental document formats;
- classification of newspaper coverage;
- schemes, departments, locations and official designations;
- preferred translation and transliteration conventions; and
- DGIPR-specific content-quality rules.

Departmental information will not be used to train a public model. Training datasets, model versions and evaluation results will remain subject to departmental control.

### 5.5 AI orchestration and MLOps

The solution will use controlled AI orchestration to connect document extraction, OCR, speech recognition, retrieval, generation, translation, image creation and video processing into auditable workflows. An **MLOps** layer will support:

- model registry and version control;
- prompt and workflow versioning;
- evaluation before significant model changes;
- inference monitoring and observability;
- controlled model upgrades and rollback;
- quality and failure-rate tracking; and
- capacity and performance monitoring.

### 5.6 AI evaluation and responsible human review

Representative DGIPR test cases will be used to evaluate retrieval relevance, source coverage, factual faithfulness, Marathi language quality, terminology consistency, translation fidelity and unsupported-claim rates. **Human-in-the-loop (HITL)** review will remain mandatory for official communication: AI supports production, HashCase performs first-level quality control and DGIPR provides final approval.

### 5.7 Security and data governance

The platform will support enterprise and government security controls, including:

- Role-Based Access Control (RBAC);
- encryption in transit and at rest;
- authenticated access and session control;
- audit logs and activity traceability;
- configurable data-retention rules;
- separation of users, roles and administrative permissions;
- source and output provenance;
- secure backup and disaster recovery; and
- restricted-network operation where required by the approved infrastructure.

## 6. Content Intake and Document Intelligence

The platform will accept the formats in which government information actually arrives:

- typed or handwritten notes;
- press notes, scheme briefs and approved articles;
- PDFs, Word documents, text files and spreadsheets;
- government resolutions, circulars, notices and annexures;
- scanned documents and photographed pages;
- meeting recordings and mobile audio;
- WhatsApp voice notes;
- YouTube links and other approved media sources; and
- multiple source materials combined into one assignment.

Born-digital PDFs will be read through their embedded text layer wherever usable. Scanned or visually encoded documents will be processed through OCR and multimodal document understanding. Required pages can be selected before OCR so irrelevant covers, blank pages and annexures are not processed unnecessarily. Extracted content will remain editable and traceable to its original page or source.

The Marathi-first interface will work across desktop and mobile layouts, provide visible progress for long-running tasks and preserve completed stages when another operation fails. Its installable mobile-web experience will make the platform practical for officers working with material received through mobile devices.

## 7. Audio and YouTube Transcription

The transcription workspace will support meetings, speeches, interviews, press conferences, voice notes and media recordings. It will provide:

- transcription of one or multiple audio files;
- transcription of YouTube videos without manual downloading;
- source-by-source results and error handling;
- editable and downloadable transcripts;
- reuse of existing transcripts in later workflows;
- direct transfer of a transcript into the article-production workflow; and
- Android sharing from WhatsApp or a phone recorder into the installed application.

The transcript will be treated as a reviewable record of what was heard. Sensitive names, quotations, dates, amounts and technical terms will be checked against the original recording before publication.

## 8. News and Article Production

The platform and on-site team will convert documents, meeting material, recordings and instructions into:

- concise factual news reports;
- detailed scheme and public-information articles;
- meeting and event coverage;
- press-note-based communication;
- explanatory and background articles; and
- Mahasamvad-style feature content.

The workflow will include source selection, editable extraction, key-point review, 5W1H analysis, detection of people and designations, verified terminology and source-faithfulness checks. Relevant historical Mahasamvad articles may guide editorial style and structure, while new facts will come only from the approved source material.

Finished articles can include the standard news dateline, confirmed names and designations, feedback-based revisions, source review, coverage verification and downloadable text, Markdown and publication-ready PDF formats.

## 9. Translation Services

The solution will provide multilingual communication for regional, national and international audiences. Services will include:

- Marathi, Hindi and English translation;
- translation into required Indian and international languages;
- translation of articles, press releases, captions, campaign material, summaries and official communication;
- language-specific review of names, places, organisations and schemes;
- preservation of dates, amounts, designations and other sensitive facts;
- verified transliteration and glossary-based terminology; and
- delivery of publication-ready multilingual versions.

The verified glossary and translation memory will improve consistency across repeated names and official expressions. Machine translation will remain subject to quality review, particularly for high-importance public communication and languages requiring specialised linguistic verification.

## 10. Proofreading and Language Correction

Proofreading will be provided as a separate service from translation. The platform will check Marathi and English material for:

- spelling;
- grammar;
- punctuation;
- language usage;
- verified-name mismatches;
- unintended changes to numbers; and
- Mahasamvad-style recommendations where applicable.

Each issue can display the original expression, suggested correction and explanation. Uncertain proper names will not be silently changed. A clean corrected version will be provided for review and use.

## 11. Social-Media Creatives, Posters and Captions

From approved source material, the solution will produce:

- creatives for X, Facebook, Instagram and other approved channels;
- Marathi social-media captions;
- article posters;
- YouTube thumbnails;
- Cabinet-decision and CMO-format creatives;
- fresh AI-generated designs; and
- creatives based on approved departmental templates.

The system will select layouts according to the volume and structure of information, helping prevent facts from being forced into an unsuitable template. DGIPR's official emblem, wordmark and footer will be applied as controlled brand layers rather than being redrawn by an image model.

A controlled master-template library will allow authorised administrators to upload, categorise, search, enable, disable and manage approved references for social creatives, article posters and YouTube thumbnails. Templates will supply visual structure only: old wording, dates, contact details, QR codes and factual claims will not be copied into new communication.

The review interface will support direct text editing, colour and layout variations, click-to-point visual feedback, local element corrections, image-level revision and restoration of previous successful versions. Approved content can be reused across platforms without entering the complete brief again.

## 12. Complete AI Video-Creation Platform

DGIPR will receive a complete video-production tool that can be used by its studio team as well as by HashCase's on-site personnel. It will convert approved information into short explainers, campaign videos, reels, shorts, status videos and web or YouTube content.

Capabilities will include:

- script generation from notes, articles or documents;
- use of an already approved script without rewriting it;
- automatic scene division and storyboard creation;
- AI-generated scene images and animated clips;
- landscape and vertical output;
- Marathi narration and voice-over generation;
- use of an approved recorded narration;
- timed subtitles and downloadable caption files;
- scene-by-scene preview and correction;
- regeneration of only the scene that requires a change;
- reassembly of approved clips without recreating them;
- DGIPR logo and branding on every scene and final video; and
- a standard DGIPR contact slate at the end.

The storyboard and scene-review stages will allow factual, visual and branding issues to be corrected before final assembly. Approved static posters can also be converted into short motion creatives while keeping official text, numbers, logos and information panels legible.

## 13. Studio Interview and Anchor-Video Production

DGIPR's studio produces the **Dilkhulaas** and **Jai Maharashtra** interview programmes, featuring ministers, officials and other guests for television and digital communication. The proposed studio-production tool will reduce repetitive post-production work and help the team convert raw recordings into broadcast, blog and social-media outputs from one workflow.

### 13.1 Pre-interview assistance

- Research briefs using authorised background material.
- Guest profile and designation verification.
- Suggested interview themes and question banks.
- Episode rundown and segment planning.
- Anchor introduction, transitions and closing script.
- Teleprompter-ready copy.

### 13.2 Interview post-production

- Import and organisation of camera, microphone and studio recordings.
- Synchronisation of audio and video sources.
- Multi-camera alignment and assisted switching between angles.
- Combination of multiple clips into one final programme.
- Detection and assisted removal of long pauses, repeated takes and unusable sections.
- Audio cleanup, noise reduction and volume normalisation.
- Colour and frame consistency assistance.
- Addition of programme intro, outro, transitions and episode identity.
- Addition of DGIPR headers, footers, logo and watermark.
- Lower-third graphics containing the speaker's verified name and designation.
- Automatic Marathi subtitles and closed-caption files.
- Review of the complete programme before final export.
- Export in the technical format required for Doordarshan or another approved broadcast destination.

### 13.3 Digital derivatives from the same interview

- Full interview video for the web and approved platforms.
- Vertical 9:16 versions for Instagram Reels and Shorts.
- Short highlights, quotations and promotional clips.
- Automatic identification of important or newsworthy segments for human review.
- Episode teaser and coming-up video.
- Interview poster and YouTube thumbnail.
- Social caption, video title, description, hashtags and publishing copy.
- Full transcript and time-coded transcript.
- Blog article, interview summary and key quotations generated from the approved transcript.
- Searchable archive of guests, subjects, episodes and transcript content.

### 13.4 News-anchor video automation

For Instagram and other social platforms, the studio team can upload an anchor's raw camera recording and use the tool to:

- join multiple recorded clips;
- remove mistakes, repeated takes and unnecessary pauses;
- add branded backgrounds, headers, footers and on-screen headlines;
- add lower thirds and information callouts;
- clean and balance the recorded voice;
- generate subtitles automatically;
- add approved background music where required;
- produce vertical and landscape versions;
- generate a caption, thumbnail and promotional copy; and
- create multiple short clips from one longer recording.

## 14. Newspaper Monitoring and Media Intelligence

The proposed media-intelligence workflow will read approved print and digital newspaper sources and convert daily coverage into structured, searchable information. It will support:

- OCR and article extraction from newspaper pages and e-papers;
- identification of DGIPR- and Government-related coverage;
- positive, negative and neutral sentiment classification;
- separate identification of Chief Minister-related news;
- department-, minister-, scheme-, district-, publication- and topic-wise segregation;
- detection of urgent, sensitive or reputationally significant coverage;
- removal of duplicate or syndicated reports;
- newspaper clipping compilation;
- category-wise PDF export;
- concise media summaries and detailed analytical reports;
- trends across publications, issues and periods; and
- a searchable historical media archive.

AI classification will accelerate monitoring, while ambiguous sentiment and high-impact reports can be escalated for human review before being included in an official analysis.

## 15. Social-Media Campaign Impact Analysis

The solution will help DGIPR measure the public impact of approved social-media campaigns across connected platforms. It will analyse available account and campaign data, including:

- reach and impressions;
- engagement, reactions, comments, shares and saves;
- video views, watch time and completion behaviour;
- follower and audience growth;
- performance by campaign, platform, topic and content format;
- organic and paid performance where authorised data is available;
- sentiment and recurring themes in public responses;
- comparison of high-performing and underperforming content;
- publishing-time and format patterns;
- campaign-level outcomes and trend visualisation; and
- evidence-based recommendations for future communication.

The output can be provided through dashboards, detailed analysis reports, presentation-ready charts and management summaries.

## 16. Fact-Checking Using Mahasamvad and Official Sources

The platform will support evidence-based fact-checking using the Mahasamvad archive, authorised government resolutions, circulars, departmental records and approved primary government sources.

The workflow will:

- identify the factual claims in submitted content;
- retrieve relevant evidence through Semantic Search and RAG;
- compare names, dates, amounts, designations, scheme details and quotations;
- detect unsupported or contradictory claims;
- distinguish current information from superseded historical information;
- attach source references to the finding; and
- classify a claim as verified, inconsistent, unsupported or requiring departmental confirmation.

The final fact-check report will show both the conclusion and its supporting evidence. Where authorised sources conflict or do not provide enough information, the system will report the uncertainty instead of inventing a resolution.

## 17. PowerPoint Presentation Production

Using information provided by DGIPR, the AI-assisted presentation workflow will produce:

- structured presentation outlines;
- Marathi, English and multilingual slide content;
- DGIPR-branded layouts;
- executive summaries and key-message slides;
- charts, timelines, process diagrams and programme summaries;
- appropriate visual suggestions and supporting imagery;
- speaker notes and talking points;
- revisions based on departmental feedback; and
- editable PPTX and PDF outputs.

The same workflow can convert long documents, programme reports, campaign results, meeting material and analytical data into concise presentations suitable for internal review or public communication.

## 18. RFP Drafting and Documentation

The solution will assist DGIPR in preparing draft Requests for Proposals using departmental requirements and relevant previous RFPs as structural references. Outputs can include:

- background and statement of requirement;
- project objectives;
- detailed scope of work;
- functional and technical requirements;
- deliverables and acceptance criteria;
- bidder eligibility requirements;
- evaluation criteria and scoring structures;
- compliance and response tables;
- reporting and documentation requirements; and
- editable Word and PDF drafts.

Semantic Search can retrieve relevant sections from the approved RFP archive, while RAG can use those sections as controlled reference material. New tender facts and requirements will come from the information supplied for the current assignment. Drafts will remain subject to departmental, procurement, financial and legal review.

## 19. Mahasamvad Knowledge Base and Institutional Memory

Previously published Mahasamvad material will become an active institutional knowledge resource. The platform will provide:

- semantic search across historical articles;
- discovery of related reporting by meaning and editorial form;
- style and structure references for new articles;
- topic timelines and historical comparison;
- evidence-backed “What Changed?” communication;
- identification of changing figures, deadlines, decisions and implementation stages;
- source-linked historical explainers and carousels; and
- preservation of departmental knowledge when staff responsibilities change.

Historical material may guide style, structure and context but will not be permitted to silently introduce old facts into a new article.

## 20. Verified Glossary and Terminology Intelligence

A shared verified glossary will maintain approved forms of:

- names of people;
- current designations and portfolios;
- places and districts;
- departments and organisations;
- schemes, missions, portals and programmes; and
- Marathi, Hindi, English and other approved language spellings.

The glossary will support generation, translation, proofreading, subtitles, lower thirds, fact-checking and search. Verified terms can be reused across future work, reducing repeated corrections and inconsistent official terminology.

## 21. General AI Work Assistant

A flexible, multimodal AI assistant will support government-office work that does not belong to a fixed production workflow. It can assist with:

- drafting letters and preliminary notes;
- summarising documents;
- explaining government resolutions in simpler language;
- answering questions about attached files;
- reading images and photographed documents;
- working with audio and YouTube material;
- preparing replies, briefs and background notes; and
- continuing multi-turn working conversations.

The assistant will operate through the same governed AI environment. Material intended for official publication will continue through the relevant article, translation, proofreading or approval workflow.

## 22. Searchable History, Reuse and Version Control

Completed work will be stored in a searchable history so authorised users can reopen previous articles, posters, captions, transcripts, translations, videos and related outputs. The system will maintain lineage between an original source and the formats produced from it.

Capabilities will include:

- related-work threads;
- reuse of approved articles for social and video outputs;
- reuse of existing transcripts without repeat processing;
- immutable poster and creative versions;
- targeted retry of only a failed stage;
- scene-level video regeneration;
- preservation of completed work when another output fails; and
- background processing with visible task progress.

## 23. Department-Level Analytics

The platform will provide management with a consolidated view of work performed through the solution, including:

- total completed outputs;
- articles, posters, captions, transcripts, translations, checks and videos;
- output mix by feature;
- daily activity trends;
- success and failure rates;
- feedback and revision rounds;
- processing activity by user-facing task;
- natural units such as pages, audio minutes, images, characters and video clips; and
- attributable service-cost estimates where applicable.

These operational analytics are separate from social-media campaign impact analysis. Operational analytics measure the work performed by the platform; campaign analytics measure how published communication performs with the public.

## 24. Quality Control and DGIPR Approval

Every official output will follow a human-supervised review model:

1. Official source material is received and organised.
2. AI performs the required extraction, retrieval, drafting, classification or production work.
3. HashCase personnel conduct first-level factual, linguistic, visual and technical checks.
4. The review-ready output is submitted to DGIPR.
5. DGIPR officers verify policy meaning, official facts and publication suitability.
6. HashCase incorporates the requested corrections.
7. DGIPR retains final approval and publication authority.

This structure combines AI speed with professional operation and departmental accountability. It also ensures that unsupported information, uncertain names or conflicting source material is surfaced for review instead of being silently accepted.

## 25. On-Site Personnel, Training and Continuous Support

One or two HashCase professionals will work on-site at a DGIPR-designated office location as the operational bridge between departmental teams and the AI platform. Their work will include:

- operating the complete solution;
- accepting and coordinating content assignments;
- producing and checking final outputs;
- supporting the news, social, translation and studio teams;
- training DGIPR personnel on self-service features;
- assisting employees when they use the platform directly;
- documenting recurring practical requirements;
- identifying additional processes that can be automated;
- communicating product improvements to the technical team; and
- helping maintain consistent end-product quality.

This continued in-office presence will ensure that the platform remains connected to DGIPR's real working practices and that the department receives completed communication rather than being left solely responsible for operating a technology product.

## 27. Conclusion

DGIPR requires more than a collection of disconnected AI tools. It requires a complete public-relations production capability that can securely receive official information, understand it, convert it into the required formats, maintain departmental language and identity, and provide finished outputs for review.

The proposed solution combines an on-premises private AI stack, Semantic Search, a vector knowledge base, Retrieval-Augmented Generation, model fine-tuning, multimodal processing, MLOps, AI evaluation and human-in-the-loop quality control with a dedicated in-office HashCase team. This creates a department-controlled capability that is both technically advanced and operationally practical.

HashCase will operate the platform, prepare the outputs, train departmental personnel, improve workflows and help discover new applications of AI. DGIPR officers will retain control over official facts, approval and publication. Together, the platform and managed service will provide a secure, scalable and complete AI-powered PR solution for government communication.
